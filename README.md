# 2026 Dark Divide 100 Race Report

My goal in writing this is that it will help me emotionally process this race and understand my subsequent thoughts as well learn from my many mistakes for future endeavors. I also want to revisit and revisit my relationship with ultrarunning as this is my 5 year anniversary of consistently running. I'm hoping this also helps out future runners as there a criminally low amount of race reports for this race on the internet and the Dark Divide Roadless Area in general being a very scantly trafficked recreation area. Apologies in advance if this is overly self-indulgent, verbose, or boring, but hopefully it is still somewhat interesting and inspires others to get out there. I am known to be a yapper.

## Context
- (newly) 25M, is this a quarter life crisis. started running with some regularity in 2021 from soccer / hiking background. ran one year XC in high school where i was chubby, out of shape, and ran nearly DFL at a 5k. I do not have a great background in running 
- Ran 2026 Dark Divide in 21:44:15: Strava: https://www.strava.com/activities/20049895765/overview

I was signed up for the Dark Divide 100 in 2025 by using the Trail Mix fund to cover my entry fee (see https://trailmixfund.org/) but unfortunately caught COVID the week of the race so I had to drop out. Luckily the RD Sean is an all time good guy and he let me transfer my entry to 2026. I didn't race much for the rest of 2025 as Dark Divide was supposed to be my A race. I ended up opting to do a C2C Wonderland attempt instead with Oliver Accola (where I DNFd at mile 60 - still with COVID lungs a week later). Oliver however was successful in a sub 24 c2c (and than a Glacier Peak Circumnavigation the weekend after!)

2026 running wise for me had started pretty poorly as I got the flu right before Deception Pass 50k (missing another race to sickness!) and broke my collarbone in February which sidelined me for 2 months (missed Chuckanut 50k!!). So I effectively missed running 3 races in a row whether due to randomly getting sick and falling off my bike in a very uncool way. I had honestly written this whole year off in terms of racing because I wasn't sure if I was gonna have any weird complications from my collarbone surgery in February. It was my first surgery ever and my first broken bone as an adult. Luckily I healed pretty fast and I was back to running with some regularity by April. Still I got really out of shape in the 2 months I was sidelined in a sling so it felt like April, May, and June were just claw back and getting back to speed months. 

## Training
Nonetheless I probably still had the most productive training block of my life this summer (outside of hiking the PCT), made possible by good training partners, outdoor access, and an abundance of stoke. Most my summer weekends included a 25+ mile long run in the mountains which barely felt like training and more like plain old fun. I also got really into CityStrides and trying to run every street in Seattle earlier this summer, which kept city running fresh when I didn't have access to the mountains on the weekdays. 

I didn't have any significant hitch hiking missions this summer but instead got really into mixing public transit, bikepacking, and trail running for weekend warrior outings. I would take public transit as far as I could within reason, start biking to the trailhead, camp at said trailhead, go for long run the next day, and than bike back. It felt like a fun and creative way to spend a weekend with a mixture of all my favorite activities: trail running, camping, and cycling. The virtues of car free living is that it will get you dummy fit if you need to get to the mountains. 

I averaged ~85-90 miles per week in the 12 weeks before the race with my peak weeks before my taper at 121, 100, and 116 mpw, all with 10k+ gain. I also bike commuted at least 50 miles a week plus whatever biking mileage I had from the weekend bike adventure I thought of. I also did a couple of mid week, between work days bikepacking trips out on the peninsula before why the hell not. 

I usually only did a single intensity session per week which more often than not ended up being track intervals with Club Northwest on Tuesday. I sometimes added a tempo later on the week once deeper into the build which was something like 3-6 miles at MP or a hills workout but I usually was a bit too lax to add a second workout. 

I had 81 hours of activity in June, 123 hours in July and 89 in August. 

I feel like I'm rambling in this training section but this truly was the best part of my summer. When the adults say its about the journey not the destination they might actually be right. So I just want to highlight a few of my marquee adventures this summer which could be considered "key sessions" but were moreso just fun trips to the mountains.

## Pre Race
- I kept the 4 days before the race pretty lowkey but still was bike commuting and doing short < 40 minute shakeouts. I did one last fun run at Tiger with Alex and Gabe 6 days before (15 miles 4k vert). Made sure to sleep a lot going into the race. I think I slept 9.5 hours Wednesday->Thursday. 
- The week before in general I tried to keep things low stress. I helped my buddy move houses on the Monday before but besides that it was mostly just work, eat, shakeout, and sit on the couch. I knew that I was getting ready as I felt a bit mentally sharper at work and I was shooting good at pool Tuesday night.
- My mentality going into the race was fuck it whatever happens, happens. I was extremely anxious about my plantar wart causing me grief as I failed to remove in the two weeks before the race (I had gotten it frozen by the doc 2.5 weeks before race day). I also had no idea really what I was getting into as there isn't much on the Dark Divide race on the internet beside the fact that it is extremely challenging. This was my first 100 miler and I think going in with very loose expectations removed a lot of potential pre race anxiety I would've had if I set out ABC goals for myself. I loosely hoped I could go sub 24, but also knew the course record was 26.5 hours and wanted to respect that so I kept that aspiration in check and instead set out just to finish.
- I rode out with Sean Thursday afternoon. We stopped at Walmart for a stopwatch for Sean and than at Roxy's Roadhouse for pre race dinner. I also shot a couple games of pool with the local shooters Justin (owner's son), Clyde (71 years old), and Tony (70 years old). I had asked Clyde if he had any advice for a young buck and he simply replied "Don't stop going hard". What a guy. You gotta play pool pre race to get your mind right. Won the last game with a nice bank shot on the 8 into the middle bag. Walked out out right after.
    - Thanks to Sean for driving us out and waiting for me when playing pool. 
- Did typical pre race things once there: set up my tent, organized two drop bags, played some hackysack, race meeting, and tried to rest. I wanted to last minute study the course more but I fell asleep to fast
- Met up with Lita (Sean's crew) and Daryn and Nick (CNW guys) once there. Some brief chats before heading off to the tent.
  
## Environmental Variables
- 45F, 85% humidity at race start. 60 degrees and cloudy mostly. A few rain showers throughout the day.
- 6:32 AM sunrise, 7:42 PM sunset

## Race
I woke up at 4am ready to go after a fitful 6 hours of sleep the night before. I last minute changed a drop bag to go to Lewis River (mile 60ish) and ate a banana and a bagel. I tried my best to use the restroom 5 minutes before the start which gave me a bit of anxiety about potentially missing the start, but there was no line and I efficiently emptied my bowels minutes before the start. 

Sean the RD counted us down from 420 to 69 to 311 to set go and than we were off. I went out with the front pack as we started on the road but dropped back a bit as soon as we got on the trail and the real climbing started. I knew that there would be folks taking it out fast and made sure to keep the speed in check in the first 50k (which is also some advice CTB gave me the day before). I let a handful of guys pass me once the climbing started and quieted my early race nerves with the internal mantra "Let God sort em out". I had listened to that Clipse record the week before the race so it was on top of mind. Felt like it was apt though. You gotta have belief in your own pace and abilities and whether or not the front guys come back you gotta just believe you'll catch up running your own race.

I soon found myself leading the conga line of headlamps along with Kyle Moss. We had some good chats and realized that we had quite a few mutuals in the Rocky Mountain Runners since he ran with them and was CO local. It was good to reminisce on my one Boulder summer and all the good connections I had made out there. I remember remarking with Kyle about the runners that went out hot and that "the race doesn't start till mile 70". I also remember making a childish quip over semantics in good form but was unneeded.

I somehow got split from the rest of the group sometime during the road climb and I started running with Lauren from BC, making steady work on the road. I was feeling good on the road still so me and Lauren cruised up ahead of the rest of the conga line. I think at this point of the race we were sitting somewhere outside the top 5 after the first 10 miles climb. This was both our first 100 mile race so we both joked that this was the beginning of the end for us as we moved efficiently up the road climb. It soon turned back to trails and I could start to see some head lamps ahead of us on the climb to come. They definitely were moving a lot slower now compared to the start. It also was finally starting to get light out and I was feeling good so I passed the two of them and dropped Lauren on the steeps. It seemed that everyone else had poles but I was feeling fine so far with the hands on knees method. Looking back I might've gotten overzealous on the effort here but I don't think my HR spiked at all and it still felt very very controlled. I brought a pop tart, jumbo honey bun, and two 40mg caff gels for this first section and ate most in the first 12 miles save for a single gel. I bought a "honey" gel from Super Jock n Jill the day before which tasted very weird because it had added salt but also had some tea extract in it which I thought could be helpful. Probably not the bestest idea to have experimented with it on race day but the 50mg caffiene sat well with me early in.

I think we passed the first aid station (Juniper) after a small descent from the top of the climb to Tongue Mountain and I grabbed 3 more gels and maybe an oreo before leaving. Very short pit stop and I kept cruising along. Most of the aid stations followed a pattern of descent to the aid and than climb back out. So like clockwork I continued along the ridge towards Sunrise Peak. This was one of the prettiest sections of the race and the fall foliage was starting to pop out. It was a short OAB climb up to Sunrise where there was a race photographer and where I saw the race leaders. I saw second place ahead along the ridge and 3rd place Nick Stenger descending Sunrise as I was climbing up. They were both 5ish minutes ahead. I repressed the urge to try and catch up and kept things in check knowing that they would come back. 

It was a short descent from Sunrise Peak down to Sunrise aid where I again grabbed a couple gels before heading out on the nice 5 mile road downhill to Dark Creek Aid station. I kept the road chill once again doing 8:30 miles. It started to rain at this section which was a bummer since it was exposed. I was anxious about having rain all week since I knew moisture is a big enemy of my plantar wart (makes it all tender and hurt more). Regardless, I got down to Dark Creek knowing it was a key section of the race where it was something like 15 miles until the next aid station. So I grabbed maybe 4 gels and some chips from this aid. I still had 2 gels from last aid for a grand total of 6 gels. This was only like 600 calories for what was 3 hours to Wilderness Aid Station. One of the gels I had was what I brought which had 40mg caffiene which I took around noon. But I also had a single flask of Tailwind from the aid. I'm not sure how many calories were in their tailwind mix as it definitely tasted watered down. I don't think I have used electrolytes in a non race setting in years so I could also just be wrong. Likely very unideal fueling but you live and learn. 

The next section was the one of the longest aid-less section. I think it was a bit shorter than other years though. I think it was only 14.5 miles without aid instead of the usual 16.5, at least that is what I overheard from another racer. It was a nice climb from Dark Creek back up to Juniper Ridge trail. I thought this was a nice grade to run up or I was just feeling really good. I passed Nick Stenger and Daryn Hutchings (fast Club Northwest guys) in this section. They were not moving as hot as before. I tried to cajole them to run with me as the downhill was about to start but got no takers. They were 2 and 3 at this point and Daryn mentioned that first place flew by them on the Dark creek road downhill. I wasn't too worried though as I was feeling marvelous. The trail dropped down to Quartz Creek and was a green tunnel for a while. It was superbly lush though. It was quite brushy in this section which made both running efficiently and staying dry a challenge. Just wet everywhere. I started to get some chaffage in the nether region and around here but was able to resolve it by just applying a tiny bit of Vaseline which I was carrying on me. I had never chafed in half tights before this but I don't think I've ever been as soggy as I was at this point in the race. Around 4 miles out from the aid station I ran out of food. I was starting to churn through the precision gels like a man possessed. I silenced my hunger as determination to get to the next aid faster. I was still having fun though, riding high on having caught the CNW boys. I got down to Wilderness Crossing right around the 8 hour mark feeling good. 

At Wilderness Crossing (mile 39) I was half hoping to see some Cowgill friends who I knew to be volunteering at this aid. However to my dismay they were not there when I got there. It would've been the morale boost I needed after going a bit hungry. I had also asked Gabe to play a pump up song for me. Apparently I missed them by 20 minutes. Who I did see was first place taking a good ole sit eating some ramen. I really had to pee so I took a piss not far off the road which seemed to surprise everyone. I told them I was a thru hiker and used to peeing everywhere. I hope they don't think I'm too crazy for this. First place quickly got up and going once seeing me. I quickly followed suit after refilling bottles and grabbing another 4 gels and two of the chews they had. I also tried to eat a PBJ with great effort on the climb out but it felt like chewing rubber. It was nice to see all the folks close behind on the descent as I started climbing back up. I saw Kyle and Nick pretty close on my tail running down and a couple of other runners. Kyle seemed still especially stoked and moving smooth.

The next section to Wright Meadows was another 13 miles which again was a lot longer than I anticipated. I quickly caught up to Adam (first place) and we started running together. We bonded over our complaints of the wetness and "the shower" we just had in the French Creek section. Adam's from Ashland and I told him the story of my time in Ashland when on the PCT. There really isn't much of story though. I just went to the yuppie co op grocery store and bought a nice oversized button up T shirt at the thrift. What I forgot to tell him was my interesting hitch out, I got a ride from this guy who was organizing a program for the homeless in Ashland while still being unhoused himself. He was supremely nice. Nice to unlock that memory. Anyways me and Adam kept cruising along. He talked about past 100s he's done and I was just happy to listen along. Eventually I dropped him on a uphill but than he came soaring back on the rolling downhill. We decided to just run with each other on the long downhill from Snagtooth. This was a strange out and back that I did not know was on course. Luckily Adam did so we didn't waste much time heading back up the climb. It was here where I saw Kyle flying down as I was jogging up. He gave me a high five and a "dude you're up a placement every single time I see you". Great vibes. 

It was bunch of rollers back up Snagtooth into a huge downhill to Wright Meadow Aid station. Saw another race photographer at Blue Lake here which was nice to see. I asked her how much further it was to the aid station and she said 4 miles to which I replied "Well fuck I'm out of food". I was half hoping she had some on her to give. Did really bad at planning the calories this race. Luckily most of the 4 miles turned into a nice forest road shortly after so it was faster moving than expected. I was excited to see my de facto crew and later on pacer Alex Weidman at this aid. I remember thinking damn he's got that shit on. He was wearing these cool army camo pants and that brown Melly knockoff he wears. He tried to offer me some of his fancy gels but I declined knowing how expensive they were and told him I only had 8 miles to the next aid station anyways. I did ask him to re tie my right shoe which had gotten loose from the wet and causing me to feel less confident on the downhill. I quickly had a blue pancake and than some bacon which tasted really really really good. I wish I carried some of the bacon out. Instead I just grabbed more gels and got some electrolyte water. To my surprise both Adam and Kyle were only 30 seconds back on the road which I never turned around to see. This put me in fight or flight so I left the aid station a bit quicker than I probably should've.

It was a technical steep descent from Wright Meadows which made me thankful for tightening up my right shoe. I felt like I was descending at a decent clip until Kyle came hot on my tail. You can tell he's a Boulder ripper. I later learned he has the second fastest time down Green Mountain. Sigh. He passed me a couple miles out of the aid station and I didn't want to both blow my quads or my ankles trying to keep up with him downhill. In retrospect I probably should've, but I was running scared for my ankles since I rolled my bad one racing White River 50k earlier this summer. So I let him go, hoping I would catch up on the big climb up to Summit Prarie where I would than pick up Weidman as a pacer. It was pretty much all steep downhill to the Lewis River aid station. I think I was only 2 minutes back by the time I was running into the aid and he was running out. I lowkey did not know the aid station at this part of the course but was happy that it longer was 12+ mile stretches. Quickly refilled fuel here and chased out after Kyle. I saw Adam moving a bit slower 5 minutes back climbing to the aid here and knew that I probably didn't need to worry about him. 

The next section of the race was really confusing to me from Lewis River to Quartz Butte as I did not really ever pay attention to this section before the race. In my mind I thought I was gonna be starting the Summit Prarie climb at any second now but in reality I had like 9 miles of rollers to get through. The climbs here weren't steep and it was rolling runnable trail but I just couldn't get myself to move well. I think my underfueling was starting to catch up to me and my feet were still wet, so every step was tender and a little painful. I was hoping to listen to music during part of the race but I accidentally locked myself out of my phone for 5 hours at the Lewis River AS (was butt dialing the wrong password all day, even with my phone in a ziplock!) so I couldn't access music nor maps. There was a very nice view of the Lewis Falls here though which I marvelled at a bit. I made my first major navigational blunder in this section at Straight Creek. It was pretty straight forward creek crossing but for some reason I thought I had to go up river up a bunch of blowdowns when the trail was simply right were I started. I tried to orient myself with the watch but it just led me further up the blowdowns. I wasted a couple minutes here before finding the trail again. Annoying but not the end of the world. I was still miffed that if I could've used my phone I would've oriented myself faster but I digress. 

I was still very confused on the next aid station and was surprised when I pulled up to Quartz Butte Aid. I surely thought the next one was Summit Prarie. But I was glad there was this aid since I had ran out of food and water. I still don't really know what happened in this section. I just kinda faded on the rollers though runnable. I think the expectation of treating myself with some music to stim to and than not being able to listen because of a phone lockout kinda bummed me out. Even so I had Weidman to look forward to at Summit Prarie so I needed to press on. It started getting pretty dark in the forest and I was really glad to have my starting headlamp with me. The feet were getting painful and I was already starting to check out from racing Kyle. I was doing calculations in my head on finishing times and negotiating my effort / pain to reward with myself. I realized I could pretty much walk it in at 3mph for a sub 24 hour finish. I hate that I even thought of that. I needed to be present in the moment but was drifting to the finish. It doesn't help that the climb up to Summit Prarie is unrelenting and terrible without poles. It was firmly power hiking grade and I just couldn't propel myself up like I was earlier in the race. The more I put weight on my feet (running uphill) the more they would hurt ever so slightly so I found myself walking most of it.

I saw a great view of Mt Adams in the evening dusk near the top of the climb though which felt pretty special. How lucky I am to see such pretty things. But was not my internal monologue than. I was just frogging it in waiting to see Weidman. It was a couple miles of downhill in the dark till I got to Summit Prarie. It was simply a lot further than I expected since I was unprepared, a shame. I saw Kyle's headlamp storming up hill from the aid station as I was running down. He was probably 5 minutes ahead of me at this point. But I kind of instantly relegated it in mind that it was sort of over. I got into the aid and finally had my first sit down all race. I was depleted severely on calories and instantly started cramping when sitting. I got PTSD when I locked up from electrolyte imbalance and muscle weakness on my LHHT YOYO attempt 3 years prior. Luckily Weidman had Gatorade on hand and I was able to delete the cool blue sugars and restore whatever salt sweat imbalance I had. Some volunteer cooked me a quesadilla in the time I was sitting there and I ate it with joy. It was my first real food since Wright Meadow. It went down much easier than the PBJ. I sat so long I was starting to shiver. But I needed to fix my feet. My only solution being changing shoes and socks. My feet were looking trenchy. About as a bad as they did when I got trench foot in NorCal on the PCT. Weidman carried me through all of this, I can't believe I was going to reject his help because I didn't want to inconvenience anyone for my own twisted suffering. It got dark and I was shellshocked sitting there and basically useless besides my usage as a vessel to walk/run from all my problems and insecurities. He tied my shoes and changed my shirt and made sure I had enough calories because he knew I wasn't getting as much as I should. I just sat there and trying to eat back my calories and get ready for the night shift. I bought a waist light the week before the race and this was my first time using it. Nothing new on race day they say. I think I finally left that aid station after 10-15 minutes of sitting there. 

I knew the race was over as I made my way out of the aid station. I reserved myself to fighting for second place and lost my will to catch Kyle. My feet were better but still hurt, the plantar wart especially. The callus having grown quite a bit from being soggy all day. Like a jackass I got my feet soaked again in a puddle not even 5 miles out from the aid station in my new dry shoes. I walked in front of Weidman because I did not want him to push me. I was tired and I just wanted to jog it in. I lied to myself saying if I hiked now maybe I would have the legs for sub 6 miles on the final 9 mile road. My mind was constantly thinking of the finish instead of the present moment. There was much more climbing in this final 27 miles than I expected. I was walking up every hill. The sky was a blanket of darkness that enveloped everything, including whatever mid race aspirations I had come up with. The stars were beautiful but I still couldn't spare any time to look up. We made a couple of wrong turns. The 11 miles from Summit Prarie and Sunrise is objectively not that challenging but I didn't spare the effort. Sorry if my writing is one big pity party here but I just really don't know what happened. Maybe I am being hard on myself but I would be lying if I said I am not disappointed in myself for not racing to the finish. I can't remember if we ever saw Kyle again in the final 27 miles. I vaguely remember him telling us that we needed to wake the aid station volunteer up at Sunrise. Weidman finally got some effort out of me after Sunrise when he said he thought he saw a headlamp behind us on the ridge. I was able to still muster some effort when he started running in front of me down Sunrise aid. I was counting every mile till the dirt road, my manufactured Valhalla where I would finally let everything rip. 

It felt like we ripped the descent down to the final aid station motivated by the fact that there was a 3rd place hot on our heals. I was surprised to reach this final aid station. To my great surprise there was the Cowgill folks here (Clayton, Gabe, and Matt) and they were playing my requested song "SkeeYee" by Sexyy Red. I think I said hi and than me Weidman left. I was so lost I couldn't even enjoy the song. They told me Kyle was 30 minutes ahead. It seemed like he was racing like a man possessed. We jogged the final 9 miles on the road in silence. I think Weidman's headlamp died. I kept looking back to check if 3rd place was behind me. I didn't see any lights behind us for a good while even though the forest was thick. I assumed I was safe. One foot in front of the other. I was jogging 8 minute miles that felt like 6:30s. Reminder to self it is hard to run 100 miles. I started getting emotional. I was happy to knowing I was gonna finish. It's been a hell of a year. I can't tell if Weidman knew that I was crying or not for half that descent. I thought about my family and friends. I was thankful for everyone who was in my life. I couldn't believe I had done it after being in a sling in April. Plantar wart be damned. I was overjoyed. Serenity overflowed me as we reached the flat road and the end was in sight. I realized I had no reason to be mad. I went in with no expectations hoping to finish and beat the previous course record by 5 hours. I had not even imagined that I would've ran under 22 hours going into the race, yet I had done it. I felt like I was sprinting as I returned back to Cispus Learning Center. In theme with my day, I went the wrong way on the final straightaway and had to hook back awkwardly to the finish line. Relief. It was over. 2nd place overall in 21:44:15. I didn't see Kyle at the finish but I was stoked for him. He ran the better (and smarter) race. Not bad for my debut 100. I chatted with Sean the RD and the folks at the finish line for a bit but it felt like a fever dream. From pictures at the end it looked like my skin looked very soft as if I came out of DongBei Spa treatment. 

It was nice to see all the Cowgill folks (Clayton, Gabe, Matt, Lita) that were back at the finish. I was fantasizing about laying down in my now wet 1 person trekking pole tent all night but learned that there were bunk beds that I could nap on. I took a shower and tried to rest. According to Clayton my feet looked like ghastly, they certainly felt as such. I was struggling to breathe. For the next hour laying there I felt like I had to fight for each breath. I was so cold yet warm at the same time. I didn't want to drink my cold water because I knew it would make me feel the chill even more. But my throat was so dry. I kept coughing. I felt bad for Gabe and Clayton trying to sleep. But felt even more bad for Matt and Weidman who went to go fetch Weidman's car 2 hours away via dirt road in the middle of the night. 

I don't really remember much from the night besides a few short conversations and seeing a couple rabbits. But I am infinitely blessed to have such a good friend Alex Weidman to accompany me for it. Weidman believed in my own abilities more than I did myself. I owe him a lot. We drove back later that morning to Seattle and I was back home before noon. I laid in bed all day. I called my mom.

## Things I could've done better
- I ran out of food multiple times at the start of the race since I was skimping out on grabbing enough gels. I really underestimated the distance between aid stations (whoops I didn't really study the course at all). I was only grabbing 3 gels at each aid station bc I had heard in past years that aid stations ran out of food so I didn't want to be too selfish. They also only put 10 gels out at a time so I would feel like an omega douche grabbing over half at once. Realizing now I needed to grab a couple more at each if I wanted to fuel a bit better. I was probably only hitting 200 calories an hour even though my stomach could handle much much more. Not running out of food > carrying less. Bad habit from thru hiking... used to carrying less in the woods and than eating my stomach in town.
